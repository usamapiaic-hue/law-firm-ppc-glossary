# Law Firm PPC Glossary

Pay-per-click reports are full of abbreviations. Partners who approve the budget often nod along without knowing which numbers matter. This glossary explains the terms in plain English, with a note on why each one matters to a law firm. Published by Legal Web Agency.

| Term | Plain meaning | Why it matters to a firm |
|------|---------------|--------------------------|
| CPC (cost per click) | What you pay each time someone clicks the ad | Legal clicks are among the most expensive; a high CPC is normal |
| CTR (click-through rate) | Share of people who saw the ad and clicked | Low CTR usually means the ad does not match the search |
| Conversion | A call, form or chat you chose to count | Only count real enquiries, not page views |
| Cost per lead | Ad spend divided by enquiries | More useful than CPC for judging an account |
| Cost per case | Ad spend divided by signed clients | The number partners should watch |
| Match type | How closely a search must match your keyword | Loose match types waste money without negative keywords |
| Negative keyword | A word that stops your ad showing | "jobs", "free", "salary" save budget from day one |
| Quality Score | Google's 1-10 rating of keyword, ad and page fit | Higher scores lower your cost per click |
| Impression share | How often your ad showed when it could have | Low share may mean the budget runs out early |
| Ad schedule | Hours and days the ads run | Run ads when someone can answer the phone |
| Call extension / call asset | A phone number shown with the ad | Many legal leads call straight from the ad |
| Landing page | The page the ad sends people to | It should match the ad, not be the homepage |

## Three numbers to ask for every month

1. Total spend.
2. Number of real enquiries (calls over 60 seconds plus forms).
3. Number of signed cases from those enquiries.
